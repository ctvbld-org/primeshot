# Professional Translation Quality Guidelines

## Primeshot Brand Voice

All translations must embody Primeshot's tone: **professional yet friendly, simple yet premium, playful yet trustworthy**. Think of a talented photographer friend who is confident in their craft, approachable in tone, respectful of privacy, and lightly playful.

### Brand Voice Principles for All Languages:
- **Clear**: Short, simple, direct language that users grasp instantly
- **Premium**: Polished but never stiff or overly formal
- **Friendly**: Approachable and warm, building connection
- **Reassuring**: Builds trust at every step of the user journey
- **Playful**: Subtle wit and charm, never cheesy or gimmicky

### Universal Content Guidelines:
- **Speak human, not technical**: Avoid AI/ML jargon; use everyday words ('headshot,' 'shoot,' 'outfit')
- **Outcome over feature**: Emphasize results (confidence, professional image), not just process
- **Respect user's photos**: Treat uploads as personal and valuable, never trivial
- **Concise always**: Users should grasp value in one glance

## Core Translation Principles

When translating content from English to target languages (French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, German, Dutch, Chinese, Japanese), adhere to these professional translation principles:

### 1. Brand Voice Adaptation

- Preserve Primeshot's friendly-professional tone in culturally appropriate ways
- Maintain the balance of premium quality with approachable language
- Adapt playfulness to cultural norms (subtle in formal cultures, warmer in casual ones)
- Ensure reassuring tone builds trust according to cultural expectations
- Keep clarity as the top priority across all cultural contexts

### 2. Accuracy and Meaning Preservation

- Preserve the original meaning, intent, and brand tone of the source text
- Maintain the same level of formality or informality as the source
- Accurately convey technical information without distortion
- Preserve emphasis, urgency, or persuasive elements from the source text
- Ensure numerical information, measurements, and data remain accurate

### 3. Natural Language Flow

- Create translations that sound natural to native speakers
- Avoid literal word-for-word translations that sound awkward or mechanical
- Follow natural sentence structure and grammar conventions of the target language
- Use idioms and expressions native to the target language when appropriate
- Adjust word order and phrasing to match what would be expected in the target language

### 4. Cultural Appropriateness and Localization

- Adapt content to be culturally relevant and sensitive to the target audience
- Consider cultural connotations of words, colors, symbols, and metaphors
- Adjust examples or references to be culturally appropriate
- Be aware of regional variations within languages (e.g., European vs. Latin American Spanish)
- Consider societal norms, taboos, and sensitivities specific to each culture

### 5. Technical Translation Considerations

- Use industry-standard terminology for photography and technology terms
- Avoid AI/ML jargon; prefer everyday language that users understand
- Maintain consistent terminology throughout the translation
- Preserve formatting, variables, and placeholder syntax (e.g., `{{name}}`, `{count}`)
- Understand and correctly translate UI/UX terminology

### 6. Contextual Awareness

- Consider the physical context where text appears (buttons, forms, headers, tooltips)
- Account for space constraints in UI elements (some languages expand when translated)
- Understand the user journey and how the text supports user actions
- Ensure calls-to-action maintain their clarity and persuasive impact
- Preserve the relationship between related text elements

## Regional Variants Policy

- All languages use simplified country codes. Examples:
  - English: en
  - Spanish: es
  - Portuguese: pt
  - Chinese Simplified: cn
  - Japanese: jp
  - French: fr
  - German: de
  - Italian: it
  - Dutch: nl
- Default reference source is en. Ensure target translations reflect en strings.
- No i18n fallback is configured. Every supported language must provide all keys for each namespace.
- Persisted user preference uses the same country codes and must be maintained consistently across files and database values.

## Language-Specific Guidelines

### French (fr)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for French Culture:**
- French users appreciate sophistication and quality; emphasize premium results with elegant simplicity
- Use "vous" for respectful professionalism while maintaining warmth through word choice
- French culture values craftsmanship; position Primeshot as artisanal quality made accessible
- Subtle playfulness works well; avoid overly casual American-style enthusiasm

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use appropriate formal ("vous") or informal ("tu") address based on context
- Be mindful of gender agreement for nouns and adjectives
- Maintain proper use of French punctuation, including spaces before double punctuation marks
- Consider character expansion (English text can expand 15-30% when translated to French)
- **Regional Variants**: Distinguish between French (France) and Canadian French
  - Avoid Canadian French terms like "téléverser" (use "importer/télécharger" instead)
  - Use "commentaires/avis" instead of "retours" for feedback
  - Prefer "construire" over "façonner" for building/shaping concepts
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "Des portraits professionnels, sans complications"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "Vos photos entre de bonnes mains"
  - Playful: "Fini les séances photo interminables"

### Spanish (es)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for Spanish Culture:**
- Spanish users value warmth and personal connection; use "tú" to build friendly rapport
- Emphasize confidence and results; Spanish culture appreciates bold, clear benefits
- Playfulness can be more expressive than in other cultures; use vivid, engaging language
- Trust is built through transparency and genuine care for user experience

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use appropriate formal ("usted") or informal ("tú") address consistently
- Pay attention to subjunctive mood usage where appropriate
- Consider regional variations if targeting specific Spanish-speaking markets
- Be mindful of gender in language and provide inclusive options where possible
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "Retratos profesionales, sin complicaciones"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "Tus fotos en las mejores manos"
  - Playful: "Adiós a las sesiones de fotos eternas"
- **Natural Phrasing**: 
  - Use "pequeño grupo" instead of "grupo reducido"
  - Prefer "crear" over "formar" for building/creating concepts
  - Use "no te convence" instead of "no encaja" for conversational tone

### Italian (it)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for Italian Culture:**
- Italians appreciate style, quality, and bella figura; emphasize elegance and professional results
- Use "tu" for warmth while maintaining sophistication through refined language choices
- Italian culture values craftsmanship and artistry; position Primeshot as bringing studio artistry to everyone
- Playfulness should be charming and stylish, never crude or overly casual

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use appropriate formal ("Lei") or informal ("tu") address based on context
- Pay attention to gender and number agreement throughout texts
- Maintain proper formatting of dates, times, and numbers according to Italian conventions
- Be attentive to false friends between English and Italian
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "Ritratti professionali, senza complicazioni"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "Le tue foto nelle mani giuste"
  - Playful: "Basta con i servizi fotografici infiniti"
- **Language Preferences**: Avoid anglicisms when Italian equivalents exist
  - Use "commenti" or "opinioni" instead of "feedback"
  - Prefer "creare" over "definire" for building/creating concepts
  - Use "quello che svilupperemo" instead of "ciò che realizzeremo" for natural flow

### Dutch (nl)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for Dutch Culture:**
- Dutch users value directness, honesty, and practical benefits; be straightforward about results
- Use "je" for friendly approachability; Dutch culture appreciates informal, genuine communication
- Emphasize efficiency and quality without unnecessary embellishment
- Playfulness should be subtle and clever; Dutch humor tends to be dry and understated

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use appropriate formal ("u") or informal ("je/jij") address based on context
- Be aware that Dutch can be more direct and concise than English
- Pay attention to word order differences, especially in subordinate clauses
- Consider separable verbs and their placement in different sentence structures
- Be mindful of false friends between English and Dutch
- Account for text expansion (English text typically expands 10-20% when translated to Dutch)
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "Professionele portretten, zonder gedoe"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "Je foto's in goede handen"
  - Playful: "Geen eindeloze fotoshoots meer"
- **Language Preferences**: Use Dutch equivalents for English business terms
  - Use "planning" instead of "roadmap" for more natural Dutch

### Portuguese (pt)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for Portuguese Culture:**
- Portuguese users appreciate warmth, personal relationships, and quality craftsmanship
- Use "você" for respectful friendliness; balance professionalism with genuine care
- Emphasize trust and reliability; Portuguese culture values long-term relationships
- Playfulness should be warm and inviting, reflecting Portuguese hospitality

**Language Guidelines:**
- Specify whether using European Portuguese (pt-PT) or Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR)
- Use appropriate formal ("você"/"o senhor"/"a senhora") or informal ("tu") address based on context and region
- Pay attention to significant differences in vocabulary and grammar between European and Brazilian Portuguese
- Be mindful of gender agreement for nouns, adjectives, and past participles
- Consider text expansion (English text can expand 15-30% when translated to Portuguese)
- Use the appropriate placement of pronouns, which differs between European and Brazilian Portuguese
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "Retratos profissionais, sem complicações"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "As suas fotos em boas mãos"
  - Playful: "Acabaram-se as sessões fotográficas intermináveis"
- **European Portuguese Natural Phrasing**:
  - Use "Agora faz parte" instead of "Passa agora a integrar"
  - Prefer "Aqui está o que precisa" over "Eis o que precisa"
  - Use "queremos saber" instead of "queremos ouvi-lo" for approachable tone
  - Replace "Como um dos" instead of "Sendo um dos" for less formal phrasing

### German (de)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for German Culture:**

- Use "du" for friendly approachability while maintaining German standards of quality

- Account for text expansion (English text can expand 20-35% when translated to German)

- **Tone Examples**:

### Chinese (zh)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for Chinese Culture:**
- Chinese users value quality, status, and face (面子); emphasize professional results that enhance personal image
- Use respectful "您" while maintaining warmth through considerate language choices
- Chinese culture appreciates efficiency and results; focus on tangible benefits and time-saving
- Playfulness should be subtle and respectful; avoid overly casual Western-style humor

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use Simplified or Traditional Chinese based on target market
- Consider cultural nuances and avoid direct translations of idioms or metaphors
- Be aware that Chinese is often more concise than English
- Pay attention to appropriate level of formality
- Consider how concepts unfamiliar to Chinese culture should be explained
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "专业肖像，简单便捷"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "您的照片，我们用心呵护"
  - Playful: "告别漫长的拍摄过程"
- **Natural Phrasing**:
  - Use "小群体用户" instead of "少量核心用户" for less corporate tone
  - Prefer "打造" over "塑造" for building/creating concepts
  - Use "告诉我们您的想法" instead of "欢迎反馈" for direct engagement
  - Replace "如有任何不便" with "如果有什么想法" for approachable tone

### Japanese (ja)

**Brand Voice Adaptation for Japanese Culture:**
- Japanese users value quality, attention to detail, and omotenashi (hospitality); emphasize careful craftsmanship
- Use appropriate keigo (polite language) while maintaining warmth through considerate expressions
- Japanese culture appreciates subtlety and refinement; position Primeshot as thoughtfully designed service
- Playfulness should be gentle and respectful; avoid direct or overly casual approaches

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use appropriate levels of formality (keigo) based on context
- Consider character limits as Japanese may be more concise than English
- Be mindful of cultural concepts that may not have direct equivalents
- Pay special attention to politeness levels and honorifics
- Structure sentences according to Japanese grammatical patterns
- **Tone Examples**:
  - Clear & Premium: "プロ品質のポートレート、シンプルに"
  - Friendly & Reassuring: "お写真を大切にお預かりします"
  - Playful: "長い撮影時間とはさようなら"
- **Balanced Politeness**:
  - Use "初期ユーザーの皆さん" instead of "早期ユーザーとしての皆さま" for natural tone
  - Prefer "作る" over "形作る" for simpler, more direct language
  - Use "始め方をご紹介します" instead of "次の手順をご確認ください" for friendlier approach
  - Replace "皆さまからの一言一言" with "皆さんの声" for appropriate politeness

## Quality Assurance Checklist

Before finalizing translations, verify that they:

- Maintain the same meaning as the source text
- Sound natural to native speakers
- Use correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation
- Preserve all variables and placeholders
- Maintain consistent terminology
- Are culturally appropriate and sensitive
- Follow the technical specifications of the project
- Meet length constraints for UI elements
- Preserve formatting, styles, and emphasis where needed
- Function correctly in context (e.g., as buttons, form labels, error messages)
- Verify that region codes are correctly used in file paths and i18n configuration.
- Ensure no keys are missing (fallback disabled).

**English Original:**
```json
{
  "buttons": {
    "uploadPhotos": "Upload Photos",
    "startSession": "Start Photo Session",
    "saveChanges": "Save Changes"
  }
}
```

